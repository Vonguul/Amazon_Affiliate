## Angle

The cheapest room to keep warm at night is the bed itself. The article works layer by layer: what goes under you (a heated
mattress pad, or a fleece or flannel layer), what goes on you (flannel sheets, a warmer comforter or a weighted or heavier blanket),
and where the summer set goes in the meantime. It stays an organization site: the last section stores the off-season bedding flat
and labelled, so the swap in March is a ten-minute job.

**Why now:** the first cold nights are in November, and people switch bedding before the heating bill arrives. A direct spin-off of
`space-heaters-heated-throws-warm-one-room` (which warms one room and already has a heated throw).

## Quality bar

- In stock, sold by Amazon or a reputable seller (the brand's own store counts).
- Rating about 4.3 or better with a real review count (hundreds, not a handful).
- ASIN not already on either site (ask for a duplicate check in a session; the guest room and space heater guides have bedding and a
  heated throw).
- For heated products: auto shut-off and a safety certification (UL, ETL or similar) stated in the listing; skip any that don't say.

## Writing notes

- Structure: one problem per section (cold mattress, cold sheets, not enough on top, the off-season set).
- No prices. No health or sleep-quality claims; for a weighted blanket, describe what it is and what the listing says, nothing more.
- Heated products: tell readers to follow the maker's instructions, never to fold or bunch a heated pad or blanket, and to check the
  shut-off setting. Do not suggest using them with infants or anyone who can't operate the controls.
- End with the swap habit: store the summer set flat and labelled by size, and do the switch on the same weekend each year.
