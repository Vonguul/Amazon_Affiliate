## Angle

The medicine cabinet fails the same way every winter: someone is sick at 2 AM and the household is digging through a drawer of
half-empty boxes, expired bottles and loose bandages. The article sorts the supplies by job, not by shelf: everyday medicines kept
visible and labelled, a first-aid kit that is actually complete, the things kids need kept out of reach, and a twice-a-year
expiry check. It stays an organization article: containers, labels and where things live. It gives no medical advice.

**Why now:** cold and flu season starts in November and people restock before the holidays. A spin-off of
`small-bathroom-storage-ideas` (which has one medicine-cabinet turntable) and of `car-trunk-organization-ideas` (which has the car
emergency kit; this is the home version).

**Internal links:** `small-bathroom-storage-ideas` (where the cabinet lives), `under-sink-organization-ideas` (cleaning products stay
separate from medicines), `car-trunk-organization-ideas` (car kit), `linen-closet-organization-ideas` (a shelf for the overflow bin).

## Quality bar

- In stock, sold by Amazon or a reputable seller (the brand's own store counts).
- Rating about 4.3 or better with a real review count (hundreds, not a handful).
- ASIN not already on either site (ask for a duplicate check in a session; the small bathroom guide has a medicine turntable and the
  car trunk and gift guides have the Everlit car kit; the gift guide also has the Brother label maker bundle).
- First-aid kit: the listing must list its contents. Skip any kit sold mainly on piece count with no contents list.

## Writing notes

- Structure: one problem per section (can't find it at 2 AM, half-empty and expired boxes, kids and visitors, the first-aid kit
  that is missing the basics, the overflow).
- No prices. No medical or health claims: the article organizes supplies and never says what to take or how to treat anything.
  Tell readers to keep products in their original packaging with the dosing instructions, to follow the label or a pharmacist, and
  to take expired medicines to a pharmacy take-back point rather than the trash (keep it general; no specific program claims).
- Bathrooms are humid; mention that many labels say to store medicines in a cool, dry place, so a bedroom or linen-closet shelf can
  be the better spot. Keep this to what labels say.
- Lock box section: frame it as keeping medicines out of reach of children, nothing more.
- End with the habit: check expiry dates twice a year on the same weekends (for example when the clocks change).
