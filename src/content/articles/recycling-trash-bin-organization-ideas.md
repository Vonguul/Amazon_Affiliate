A kitchen actually produces three separate waste streams — trash, recyclables, and food scraps — but most kitchens only have a system for one of them (usually just trash), which is why recycling and compost end up as an afterthought pile instead of an actual habit. Building in a real spot for each stream, rather than one bin and good intentions, is what makes sorting something that happens automatically.

## Cabinet space decides the format

A kitchen with an existing cabinet built around one trash can has real options — a slide-out system that fits two bins side by side turns that same cabinet into a trash-and-recycling station without adding any new footprint to the room.

## No cabinet space means a freestanding sorter

Not every kitchen has a cabinet built around this problem, and retrofitting one isn't always realistic. A freestanding multi-bin sorter solves the same problem in a corner, a mudroom, or a garage — the tradeoff is floor space instead of cabinet space, but the sorting habit works the same either way.

## Compost is a third stream, not an extension of trash

Food scraps thrown in with regular trash just add smell and weight to a bag that's headed for a landfill anyway. A small, sealed counter compost bin gives that stream its own home — one with an actual charcoal filter, so it doesn't sit out smelling up the kitchen the way an open bowl does.
