Outdoor clutter comes in three kinds, and each needs a different answer: soft things that can't stay out in the wet, a hose that ends up in a pile by the spigot, and long-handled tools that lean against whatever's nearby. A single big box won't solve all three.

## Size the deck box to the cushions, not the patio

The usual mistake is buying a box that looks right on the patio and then finding the cushions don't fit. Stack your cushions and measure them first, then compare with the box's inside dimensions, which are smaller than the outside ones. A full set of seat cushions points to one of the big boxes. If all you need to hide is gloves and small tools, a 30-gallon box does the job and gives you a side table too.

Check the listing's wording as well. These boxes are sold as weather-resistant, which isn't the same as sealed, so it's safest to keep anything you'd hate to see damp somewhere else.

## Give the hose a wall

A hose left on the ground gets kicked, kinked and run over. Hanging it within reach of the spigot makes coiling it about as easy as dropping it. Pick the hanger by whether you want somewhere to keep the nozzle: the enclosed one handles that, and the metal one simply looks better on a wall people see.

## Stand the tools up

Rakes and shovels fall over because nothing holds them. A tower keeps each one upright and separate, and wheels mean it can go to the far side of the garage when the car needs the room. For wall-mounted options and the rest of that space, see [garage organization ideas](/articles/garage-organization-ideas/).

## Decide where cushions spend the winter

If you have indoor space, large zippered bags are a cheaper way to keep cushions clean through the off-season than a second deck box. They suit a garage shelf or the back of a closet. The trade-off is that you need that indoor space, which is exactly what a deck box saves you from needing.

## Keep seasonal gear together

Seed trays are out for a few weeks and then in the way for the rest of the year. One matching set stacks far better than a collection of odd trays. If you'd rather grow all year, there's a separate guide to [indoor growing systems](/articles/indoor-growing-systems-guide/).
