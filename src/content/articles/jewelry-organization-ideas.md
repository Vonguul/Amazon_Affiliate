Jewelry doesn't take up much room, which is exactly why it's so easy to let it pile into one drawer, dish, or tangled heap. Necklaces knot themselves against bracelets, stud earrings go missing one at a time, and the jewelry worn most often ends up buried under the pieces worn once. The four fixes below tackle it by giving each type of jewelry — everyday pieces, closet overflow, studs, and drawer jewelry — its own dedicated spot instead of one shared pile.

## Keep everyday pieces out where you'll actually wear them

The jewelry worn most often is usually the jewelry left sitting out on a dresser, because putting it away in a box means digging for it again the next morning. A wall-mounted rack keeps a rotation of everyday pieces visible and within reach, so grabbing something on the way out the door doesn't mean opening anything first.

## Give closet overflow a spot on the back of the door

A jewelry box only has so many compartments, and everything past that ends up piled on top or crammed into a drawer. A double-sided hanging organizer with dozens of clear pockets and metal hooks turns the back of a closet door — space that isn't doing anything else — into storage for the rest of the collection.

## Stop losing one earring out of every pair

Stud earrings are the easiest piece of jewelry to lose, because there's rarely a spot smaller than an entire jewelry box built specifically for them. A stand with rows of individual holes keeps every pair matched and visible at a glance, instead of loose and separated in the bottom of a dish.

## Divide the drawer instead of piling it

A drawer without dividers turns into one solid tangle the moment two necklaces or a handful of bracelets touch. Stackable velvet trays with fixed compartments keep rings, bracelets, and earrings in their own separate space, so opening the drawer means picking a piece instead of untangling one first.
