A freezer gets disorganized differently depending on which kind it is — a chest freezer buries things under their own depth, while an upright freezer lets things slide and tip on open shelves. Both end in the same place (frozen mystery bags, food that expired without anyone noticing), but the actual fix depends on which failure mode you're dealing with.

## Chest freezers fail from depth, not clutter

The bottom of a chest freezer is effectively invisible once anything's stacked on top of it, which is how food ends up frozen solid for a year without anyone remembering it exists. Baskets that keep items near the top and pull straight up solve the actual problem — visibility — rather than just re-stacking the same depth issue in a bin.

## Upright freezers fail from sliding, not depth

A shelf in an upright freezer is fully visible, but nothing on it stays put — bags slide to the back, boxes tip over, and one shelf slowly turns into a single merged pile. Bins that act like drawers, sliding in and out as a unit, keep that shelf-wide pile from forming in the first place.

## Label before freezing, not after

Once something's frozen, "what is this and how old is it" becomes nearly impossible to answer just by looking. A date and a name written on a removable label before it goes in the freezer is the cheapest single habit that prevents the mystery-bag problem, and it takes seconds compared to guessing later.

## Uniform shapes stack better than whatever's on hand

Mismatched shapes — a takeout container here, a random freezer bag there — leave gaps that waste real freezer volume even when there's technically room. Standardizing on one stackable container shape is what actually recovers that wasted space, the same principle a small pantry uses with matching containers on the shelf.
