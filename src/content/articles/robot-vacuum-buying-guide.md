A robot vacuum is a purchase where the gap between a cheap one and a good one is real. The cheap ones tend to bump into things, miss corners, and need rescuing from a sock. The better ones map the house, avoid cables and pet messes, and empty and wash themselves, which is where the extra money goes.

This guide is organized by home type, not by a single best pick, because the right robot depends on your floors, your pets, and how much maintenance you want to do.

## Decide on floors first

Hard floors mostly need good edge cleaning and a mop. Carpet needs suction and a brush that doesn't clog. A robot that does both well is the expensive kind. If your home is mostly one or the other, you can spend less. RTINGS publishes separate rankings for carpet, hardwood and pet hair, which is useful because no single model leads all of them.

## A self-emptying dock is the feature that changes daily life

The difference between a robot you run and forget and one you babysit is the dock. A self-emptying dock stores the dust for weeks, and a self-washing dock handles the mop pads. Both cost more, but they remove the chore that makes people stop using a robot.

## Budget: the Q7 M5+

If you want to try a robot without a large spend, the Q7 M5+ has the biggest review base of anything here and a self-emptying dock. It's best on hard floors in a smaller home.

## Mid-range: Qrevo 2 Pro, P10 Pro Gen2 and X50 Ultra

These are the sweet spot for most homes. The Qrevo 2 Pro suits pets and mixed floors. The MOVA is attractive if mopping is the priority, with the caveat of a smaller review base. The X50 Ultra suits homes with door tracks and thresholds that stop other robots.

## Premium: Saros 10R and Saros 20

The Saros 10R is the one RTINGS rated highest in its tests, and the Saros 20 is the larger step up in obstacle handling and mop washing. The premium tier is justified by a large home with mixed floors, or by wanting the least intervention. For a small apartment, it is more robot than you need.

## Worth being direct about

Robot vacuums are not a replacement for a full vacuum. Consumer Reports notes that an upright or canister vacuum is still better for deep cleaning. Ratings on Amazon sit lower than the best lab results for several of these models, so read the recent reviews for the specific listing, and check the return window. Models are refreshed often, so a listing may be replaced by a newer version.
