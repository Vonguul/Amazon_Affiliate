Holiday hosting has two halves that rarely get planned together. The first is the day itself: where everyone sits, how the food stays warm, and where the coats go. The second is the other eleven months, when the extra table, the chairs, the serving pieces and the good dishes all have to live somewhere without getting in the way or getting chipped.

Everything in this guide is chosen to solve both halves: it works for the meal and then folds, stacks or zips away.

## Seating first

The table is the one thing you can't improvise. A 6-foot fold-in-half table seats six to eight and folds into a slab you can carry by its handle and slide behind a door. Pair it with padded folding chairs rather than the hard plastic kind. Guests sit at a holiday table for two or three hours, and hard chairs end the conversation early.

## Keep the food warm without the oven

Once the oven is full, chafing dishes are how caterers keep a buffet warm, and a home set works the same way. They need canned chafing fuel underneath. Light it only in a room with good airflow, keep it away from anything that can catch, and never leave it burning unattended.

## Give the coats a place before the doorbell rings

The coat pile on the guest bed is a holiday tradition nobody enjoys. A rolling garment rack set up in the entry before guests arrive gives every coat a hanger and saves the end-of-night search through the pile.

## Store the good dishes so they survive until next year

Fine china and stemware rarely break during the meal. They break in storage: plates stacked without anything between them, glasses knocking together on a shelf. Padded cases with separators solve that, and they also make the whole set easy to carry to the table and back. For more on the serveware you use year-round, see [wine and bar cart organization](/articles/wine-bar-cart-organization-ideas/).

## Worth being direct about

Folding furniture is a compromise. A folding table won't feel like your dining table, and padded folding chairs are comfortable for an evening, not all day. Check each listing's measurements against your space before you buy, especially the stemware case against your tallest glasses. Holiday stock moves quickly, so if something you want is out, the other pick in the same category does the same job.
