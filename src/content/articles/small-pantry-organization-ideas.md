Small pantries fail for one reason more than any other: everything on the shelf is a different shape. Bags slump, boxes get shoved sideways, and the deep back corner becomes storage for things you forget you own. None of that is a space problem — it's a containment problem, and it's fixable without a renovation.

## Start with containment, not shelving

Before buying any new shelving or drawer inserts, standardize what's already in your pantry. Swapping bagged and boxed dry goods into uniform airtight containers does two things immediately: it makes every item the same width (so shelves stop wasting space around odd shapes), and it makes contents visible at a glance, which cuts down on the doubled-up grocery buys everyone with a messy pantry ends up making.

## Solve the dead corner

Almost every small pantry has one shelf that's deeper than it is tall, with a back third nobody can comfortably reach. Instead of losing that space, a turntable keeps everything spinnable within reach — the classic fix for oils, spice jars, and condiments that would otherwise migrate to the back and get forgotten.

## Group, don't just store

Once containment and the dead corner are handled, the last piece is grouping. Loose snack bags, baking supplies, and lunchbox items each do better in their own labeled bin that can be pulled straight off the shelf, rather than left to spread across whatever open space is left.

## Where the rest of this list fits in

Containment, the dead corner, and grouping are the three categories — everything below fills in the specifics: pantry-door space, canned goods, spices, high-volume pours, under-shelf gaps, awkward box shapes, and the labels that make containment actually work day to day. Start with the first three if you're only fixing one thing at a time; the rest layers on as needed.
