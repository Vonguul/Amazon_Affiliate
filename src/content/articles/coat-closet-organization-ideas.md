A coat closet usually doesn't run out of space. It runs out of structure. Heavy coats, wet boots, and a pile of gloves and hats all share one rod and one floor, and every family member adds a layer each winter. The fix is to give each kind of thing its own spot: the rod for coats, the door for daily grab-and-go items, the floor for boots, and the shelf for everything small.

## Start with the hangers

Thin wire and plastic hangers bend under a winter coat, and the coat slips onto the floor. Wide wooden hangers hold the shoulders, keep coats from bunching together, and make the rod look like the same closet every day. Swapping every hanger at once is the single change that most of the closet depends on.

## Use the back of the door

The inside of the door is the most-used and least-used space in a coat closet. A hook rack takes the bag, scarf or light jacket that gets grabbed every morning, so the rod is left for coats. If the door has to hold small items instead, a fabric organizer with shelves and pockets does that job. Pick one per door, and check the door's thickness and the gap at the top before buying either.

## Give the boots a tray

Snow and mud melt off boots and spread across whatever else is on the closet floor. A tray with a raised edge keeps it contained and goes in the sink or outside for a rinse. For a deeper look at drying wet gear, see [winter gear storage](/articles/winter-gear-storage-ideas/).

## One spot for gloves and hats

Gloves and hats go missing because they have no home and end up in coat pockets, on the floor or on a shelf no one can reach. Give each person their own pocket on a door organizer, or one basket in a hanging shelf unit. When the closet door can't take anything, the hanging unit hangs from the rod instead.

## Divide the top shelf

The shelf above the rod collects bags, hats and off-season bins until it's one heap that falls when anything is pulled out. Clip-on dividers turn a wire shelf into sections so each stack stands up by itself.

## Move out what's not in season

The easiest space to free up is the space taken by coats nobody is wearing this month. Vacuum bags shrink off-season jackets so they fit under a bed or on a high shelf. Puffy synthetic jackets compress well; check the care label before compressing a wool or down coat for months at a time. For more ideas by the door, see [entryway organization](/articles/entryway-organization-ideas/).

## Worth being direct about

Measure before buying anything that hangs on the door: these racks and organizers list a maximum door thickness and need a small gap at the top of the door to close properly. Weight ratings and capacities come from the makers. If your closet has solid wood shelving rather than wire, the clip-on dividers won't fit.
