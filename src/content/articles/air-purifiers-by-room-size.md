Air purifier listings on Amazon promise coverage like "up to 1,748 sq ft" for a machine that's really meant for a bedroom. Those numbers aren't false, but they're based on cleaning a room's air once an hour. Industry testing assumes about five times an hour, which means a room roughly one fifth that size.

This guide sizes every pick by the number that tells you how much air it actually cleans, and puts them in order from the smallest room to the largest.

## The one number to read: CADR

CADR (clean air delivery rate) is measured in cubic feet per minute under a standard test run by AHAM, the appliance manufacturers' association. Each purifier gets three figures, for smoke, dust and pollen. Smoke is the hardest to filter, so it's the one to size by.

The rule of thumb AHAM gives is simple: the smoke CADR should be at least two thirds of the room's floor area. For a 300 sq ft bedroom, that means a smoke CADR of 200 or more. Put another way, the room size a purifier is meant for is about 1.55 times its smoke CADR, and that is the figure used for each pick below.

To convert an Amazon "up to" figure back to a usable room size, divide it by about 5. The Coway Mighty's 1,748 sq ft works out to about 360 sq ft, which matches its AHAM rating.

## Bedrooms and small offices: about 360 to 390 sq ft

The Coway Mighty, Levoit Core 400S, Blueair 311i Max and Winix 5530 all land in the same class. They're close enough on cleaning power that the choice comes down to everything else. The Coway has the longest track record. The Levoit and Winix are app-controlled. The Blueair is the one to look at if noise in a bedroom matters most.

## Larger rooms: 465 sq ft and up

The Honeywell HPA300 suits a large bedroom or a family room, and it's the most straightforward to use. The Coway Airmega 400 and Levoit Core 600S are for living rooms and open-plan spaces. If you're choosing between those two, the Levoit moves more air for less money, while the Coway has a dual-filter design and a longer reputation.

## Go one size up

A purifier running on its top setting cleans the most air, and it's also at its loudest. Most people turn it down. Buying one size larger than your room means it can run on a quieter setting and still keep up.

## Worth being direct about

An air purifier filters particles such as dust, smoke, pollen and pet dander from the air that passes through it. It doesn't fix a source of pollution in the room, and it isn't a medical device. If you're buying one for a health condition, ask your doctor what it can realistically do for you. Replacement filters are an ongoing cost, so check the price and how often the maker says to replace them before you choose a model.
