A home gym rarely gets its own room. It's a corner of a bedroom, a slice of the garage, or a patch of living room floor that has to reset back into normal life between workouts, and the equipment that makes that possible is exactly the kind that resists being put away — dumbbells that roll, bands that tangle, mats that won't stay rolled. The four fixes below are built around getting everything off the floor and onto a wall or a rack, so the space can go back to being a bedroom or a living room in the time it takes to walk away.

## Put weights and mats on wheels, not the floor

A home gym that shares space with the rest of the house can't have equipment scattered across the floor once a workout ends. A rolling rack that holds dumbbells, kettlebells, and mats together turns cleanup into pushing one cart into a corner instead of relocating a dozen separate items by hand.

## Give bands a place to hang before they tangle

Resistance bands end up in a knotted pile at the bottom of a bag or bin faster than almost any other piece of equipment, mostly because there's rarely a dedicated place to hang them. A wall rack with separated prongs keeps each band untangled and visible, so grabbing the right resistance level doesn't mean untying three others first.

## Get mats off the floor and onto the wall

A mat leaned in a corner falls over on its own on a regular basis, then slowly unrolls itself across the floor it was supposed to be cleared from. A wall-mounted holder keeps several mats stacked flat and upright, so they take up a few inches of wall instead of a few square feet of floor.

## Store plates vertically instead of stacking them

Weight plates stacked flat on the floor are both a tripping hazard and the least space-efficient way to store anything round and heavy. Pegs mounted directly on the wall hold plates on edge, turning empty wall space into storage that the floor never has to give up.
