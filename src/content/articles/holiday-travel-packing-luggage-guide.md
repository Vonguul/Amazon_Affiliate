Holiday trips are short, busy and full of gifts going one way or the other. That makes them the trips where checked bags cost the most time and money. The aim here is a carry-on and a personal item that hold a week, packed so you can find things, and weighed before you leave.

If you're the one hosting this year, see our [guest room guide](/articles/guest-room-ready-for-holiday-visitors/) instead.

## Check your airline's size limit first

Carry-on limits vary by airline, and "airline approved" in a listing isn't a guarantee for yours. Look up the limit, then compare it with the bag's size **including wheels and handles**. Expandable bags are handy, but expanded they can go over the limit, so pack to the unexpanded size if your airline is strict.

## Four carry-ons

- **Travelpro Platinum Elite:** the premium choice for people who fly often, with self-aligning wheels and a four-stop handle.

Add an underseat backpack as your personal item and you have room for a week without checking a bag.

## Pack by category, in cubes

Packing cubes don't make clothes smaller (compression cubes do, a bit). What they do is turn a suitcase into a set of drawers: one cube for tops, one for bottoms, one for underwear and socks, a small one for chargers. You can find anything without unpacking, and the same cubes go straight into a hotel drawer.

## Small things that save trouble

- A **hanging toiletry bag** keeps a crowded guest bathroom counter clear.
- A **jewelry roll** stops chains tangling. At home, the fixes in our [jewelry organization guide](/articles/jewelry-organization-ideas/) take over.
- A **luggage scale** catches an overweight bag at home, where you can still move things to your carry-on. It matters most on the way back, when you're carrying gifts.
- An **AirTag** in each bag shows where it is if it doesn't come off the carousel.

## Worth being direct about

Suitcase dimensions and capacity are the makers' figures, and listings that say "fits most airlines" can't know your airline's current rules. Compression cubes save space, not weight. An AirTag shows a bag's location; the airline still has to return it. Ratings for some suitcases are shared across sizes and colors on the same listing. Check carry-on limits again before each trip, since airlines change them.
