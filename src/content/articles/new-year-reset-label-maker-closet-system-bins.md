The room-by-room organizing guides on this site fix one drawer or one shelf at a time. A new year is a good moment for the bigger, one-weekend projects: a whole closet, and the bins for everything stored out of sight. The order you do them in matters more than which products you buy.

## First: declutter, then measure

Every organizing project goes better if you take things out before you buy anything to put them in. Empty the closet, decide what stays, then measure the width, depth and height. Bins and closet kits come in fixed sizes, and buying them first is how people end up organizing things they meant to give away.

## Second: the closet system

Closet systems come in two kinds. Telescoping kits like Rubbermaid Configurations mount to the wall but adjust in width with telescoping rods and expandable shelves, so they need no cutting. Wall-mounted systems like ClosetMaid ShelfTrack hang from a track on the wall and let you move shelves and rods up and down, but may need cutting to fit. Measure the closet's width and buy the kit whose range it falls in. For smaller fixes, see [small closet and bedroom organization](/articles/small-bedroom-closet-organization-ideas/).

## Third: clear bins, all the same

For the things that live out of sight, clear bins beat opaque ones because you can see what's inside without opening them. Buy a matched set of one size from one brand, so every lid fits every bin and they stack squarely. Use medium bins on closet shelves and large totes in the garage. See [garage organization](/articles/garage-organization-ideas/) and [holiday decor storage](/articles/holiday-seasonal-decor-storage-ideas/).

## Last: label everything

A label is what keeps a system working in March. Label the short end of every bin, so you can read it when bins are stacked on a shelf, and label closet shelves by category. A handheld label maker is the quickest way to do it, and it's useful in [the home office](/articles/home-office-organization-ideas/) too.

## Worth being direct about

A closet system or a pile of new bins doesn't keep anything organized by itself. Habits do. Check each kit's listed dimensions against your own measurements, and remember that clear bins show clutter as well as contents.
