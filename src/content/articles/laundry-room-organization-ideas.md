A laundry room is small in almost every home, which means every square foot of it needs to earn its place. The five fixes below target the specific things that eat up space in a small laundry room without adding real function: floor-standing baskets, a drying rack that's only sometimes needed, mismatched detergent packaging, an ironing board leaned in a corner, and the narrow dead gap next to the machines.

## Sort as you go, not on wash day

A single hamper means sorting happens all at once, right when you're trying to actually do laundry. A 3-bag sorter spreads that work out — clothes get sorted the moment they're set down, so wash day is just carrying a bag to the machine.

## Let the drying rack disappear when it's not needed

A permanent floor-standing drying rack is one of the biggest space costs in a small laundry room, especially since it's often only in use part of the week. Wall-mounted and foldable, it's there when needed and flat against the wall the rest of the time.

## Give detergent and pods a matching system

Every brand of detergent, pods, and dryer sheets comes in a differently sized, differently shaped package. Decanting into matching labeled containers isn't just aesthetic — it makes it obvious at a glance when something's running low.

## Get the ironing board off the floor

A leaned ironing board takes up floor space and is genuinely a tip-over risk. Wall-mounted, it's out of the way, and a version with a small shelf and hooks handles the iron and spray starch too instead of leaving them somewhere else.

## Use the gap next to the machines

The narrow strip of space beside a washer or dryer is almost always empty — too narrow for regular furniture, easy to forget about. A cart built specifically for that width turns dead space into extra storage that rolls out when needed.
