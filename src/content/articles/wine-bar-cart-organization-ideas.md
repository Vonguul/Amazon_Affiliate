A bar cart looks cluttered almost immediately for one specific reason: bottles, glasses, tools, and garnishes all get set down on whatever open shelf is closest instead of each having an assigned spot. A cart has real structure to work with — multiple tiers, hooks, sometimes a shelf underneath — and using that structure on purpose is the actual fix.

## Give bottles a rack, not a shelf

A bottle set flat on a bare shelf rolls, takes up more linear space than it needs to, and — for wine specifically — dries out a cork faster than storing it on its side does. A small dedicated rack solves the space and the rolling problem in one step, and it looks intentional instead of like bottles just got parked there.

## Standing spirits need visibility, not just storage

Liquor bottles are usually taller and meant to stand upright, which creates a different problem: a second row hides completely behind the first. A stepped display shelf keeps every label facing forward and visible at a glance, which matters more than it sounds like it should when you're actually trying to make a drink someone asked for.

## Glasses belong underneath, not on top

Stemmed glasses sitting upright on a shelf are both wasted vertical space and one bump away from tipping over. Hanging them stem-down from the underside of a shelf clears that space entirely and keeps them more secure than standing them ever does.

## Garnishes and tools need a landing spot at the cart itself

The last failure point isn't storage capacity, it's proximity — a shaker or a jar of cocktail cherries kept in a kitchen drawer three steps away just doesn't get put back at the cart consistently. A small caddy mounted to or set on the cart keeps the actual mixing tools where the mixing happens.
