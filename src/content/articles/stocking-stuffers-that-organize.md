The best stocking stuffers get used every day. A small organizing gift works because it fixes one specific, familiar annoyance: the knot of chargers at the bottom of a bag, the necklace chains tangled after a trip, the keys that jab through a pocket. None of these is expensive or exciting on its own, and that's the point: they're the kind of thing people don't buy for themselves.

## For the traveler

A tech pouch is the one gift almost everyone uses. Cables, chargers and earbuds each get their own loop or pocket instead of one tangle. Pair it with a travel jewelry case or a hanging toiletry bag for someone who travels for the holidays. For packing the rest of the suitcase, see [holiday travel packing](/articles/holiday-travel-packing-luggage-guide/).

## For the person with the jingling keys

A key organizer folds keys into one slim stack. It's a small change that people notice every time they reach into a pocket.

## For the DIYer

A magnetic wristband is a classic stocking stuffer because it's cheap, clever and immediately useful. Screws and bits stay on your wrist instead of rolling off the top of the ladder.

## For the desk or the nightstand

Cord clips stop the charging cable from sliding behind the desk or bed every time it's unplugged. They also make a good add-on to a larger gift. For the full cable cleanup, see [cord and cable management](/articles/cord-cable-management-ideas/), and for bigger organizing gifts, see [gift ideas for people who love organizing](/articles/gift-ideas-for-people-who-love-organizing/).

## Worth being direct about

"Stocking stuffer" is a gift idea, not a size, so check the listed dimensions before you buy: the larger electronics case won't fit a stocking. Capacity claims, like the number of necklaces a jewelry organizer holds, come from the makers. Holiday shipping slows in December, so order early if a gift has to arrive on time.
