Heating a whole house to keep one room comfortable is the expensive way to stay warm. A space heater in the room you're actually using, or a heated throw on the couch, lets you keep the thermostat lower everywhere else.

This guide covers six heaters, from a small desk unit to an oil-filled radiator, and three heated throws, with the safety rules that matter more than any feature.

## Every 1500W heater makes about the same heat

Plug-in heaters in the US top out at 1500 watts, because that's what a standard household circuit can safely supply. A 1500W heater turns that power into about the same amount of heat whatever its shape or brand. What differs is how the heat reaches you: a fan heater blows it at you quickly, an oil-filled radiator warms the room slowly and evenly, and "large room" claims are mostly about airflow and oscillation.

So choose by the room and how you'll use it, not by the wattage.

## Ceramic fan heaters: fast, for rooms you're in now

The DREO and Lasko heaters are ceramic fan heaters. They warm the air near you within a minute or two, which suits a home office in the morning or a cold bathroom before a shower. The small DREO is for a desk or a corner, the Heater 319 is the quietest for a bedroom, and the Lasko tower and 16-inch DREO spread heat further across a living room.

## Oil-filled radiators: slow, silent, for overnight

An oil-filled radiator has no fan. It heats sealed oil inside the fins, which then radiates warmth for a while even after the thermostat clicks off. It takes longer to warm a room, but it's silent and its surface stays cooler to the touch than a bare heating element, which is why it's the usual choice for a bedroom overnight. The Dragon is the faster-warming, more programmable version.

## Heated throws: warm the person, not the room

A heated throw uses a small fraction of a heater's power, because it only warms you. For an evening on the couch it's often all you need. All three here have auto shut-off timers. Don't fold or bunch a heated throw while it's on, and follow the washing instructions, which always mean unplugging the controller first.

## The safety rules that matter

- Plug a heater straight into a wall outlet. Never use an extension cord or power strip: they aren't built for a heater's continuous load.
- Keep it at least 3 feet from anything that can burn: curtains, bedding, sofas, paper.
- Put it on a hard, level floor, out of walkways, where kids and pets can't knock it over. Tip-over shutoff is a backstop, not a reason to place it carelessly.
- Turn it off when you leave the room or go to sleep, unless the manual says it's designed for unattended use.
- Heat dries the air. If your rooms feel dry in winter, the humidity monitor in our [humidifiers by room size guide](/articles/humidifiers-by-room-size/) will tell you whether they are.

## Worth being direct about

A space heater saves money only if you lower the central thermostat while you use it. Run both at full and you've added cost, not cut it. Coverage figures in heater listings are the makers' own, and no independent standard exists for them the way one does for air purifiers. Heated throws aren't a medical device, so if you're buying one for a health reason, ask your doctor first. Check each listing's current safety certifications and warranty before buying.
