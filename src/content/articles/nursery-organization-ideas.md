A nursery has a specific organizing problem most rooms don't: nearly everything in it is temporary. Clothes stop fitting in a matter of weeks, care items get relocated for every diaper change, and the room itself needs to keep working as the baby grows. The four fixes below are built around that constant turnover instead of a system meant to stay fixed in place.

## Let the diaper station travel with the baby

A fixed changing station only works if the baby stays near it, which isn't realistic for most of the day. A portable caddy carries diapers, wipes, and a spare outfit wherever they're actually needed, so a diaper change doesn't mean walking back to one specific room first.

## Use the one wall that's usually empty

A nursery closet fills up with things that don't hang well — lotions, small toys, spare crib sheets — and floor space disappears fast. The back of the door is space nobody's using, and a multi-shelf organizer turns it into real storage without touching a single square foot of the room itself.

## Label clothes by the age they actually fit

Baby clothes move through sizes every couple of months, and a single crowded rod makes it easy to keep reaching for things that stopped fitting weeks ago. Dividers labeled by age turn "what actually fits right now" into something visible at a glance instead of a guess.

## Get stuffed animals off the floor for good

Stuffed animals are bulky and irregularly shaped, which makes them nearly impossible to bin the way other toys can be. A corner hammock uses wall space that's otherwise sitting empty and keeps the whole collection contained without eating into floor or shelf space.
