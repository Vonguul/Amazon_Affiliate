A messy drawer rarely starts out messy — it starts as a normal drawer with nothing inside it to keep items from drifting into one pile the moment it's closed a few times. Socks lose their pairs, small items disappear into corners, and folded stacks lean over the moment one item gets pulled from the middle. The four fixes below give a drawer or shelf actual structure instead of asking it to stay organized on its own.

## Give small items their own compartments

A drawer full of small items in a range of sizes turns into one pile the instant nothing separates them from each other. A set of bins in four different sizes lets every item get its own compartment, arranged to fit the actual drawer instead of forcing everything into a fixed layout that doesn't match.

## Split a drawer into honest sections

Not every drawer needs a full grid of small bins — some just need to be divided into a handful of real sections instead of staying one open space where everything runs together. Expandable dividers wedge securely at any width, splitting a single drawer into as many zones as it actually needs.

## Keep socks and underwear from becoming one pile

Socks and underwear are the two categories most likely to end up tangled together, since individual pieces are too small and too numerous to stay separated without help. Individual fabric boxes keep pairs matched and stop everything else in the drawer from migrating into the wrong pile.

## Stop one stack from taking down the rest

A stack of folded sweaters or jeans on an open shelf leans over the moment one item gets pulled from the middle, and it usually takes the rest of the stack down with it. Clear dividers wall off each stack individually, so removing one item — or one whole section — doesn't collapse the stacks sitting next to it.
