Winter air feels dry because cold air holds very little moisture, and heating it up makes the relative humidity drop even further. A humidifier puts some of that moisture back. Before you buy one, it's worth knowing whether you need one, and which kind suits the room.

## Measure first

A humidity monitor is the most useful thing in this guide, and the cheapest. The EPA recommends keeping indoor humidity between 30% and 50%. Below that, the air feels dry. Above it, you start to invite condensation on windows, dust mites and mold. A monitor tells you which side of that range you're on, so you only run a humidifier when you actually need it and stop before it's too much.

## Cool mist or warm mist

Most humidifiers sold today are ultrasonic cool-mist units. A vibrating plate turns water into a fine mist, quietly and with little energy. The drawback is that whatever minerals are in your tap water go into the air too, and can settle as a fine white dust on furniture. Using distilled water stops that.

Warm-mist humidifiers boil the water, so minerals stay behind in the unit and there's no white dust. They use more electricity, and the water is hot, which makes them a poor choice in a room with small children or pets. The Levoit LV600S does both, which is part of why it's the lead pick here.

## Sizing: tank and output, not the box's square footage

There's no independent standard for humidifier coverage like the clean-air rating for air purifiers, so treat "up to X sq ft" claims on the box loosely. In practice, size by tank: a 2.5 to 4 liter unit suits a bedroom, and 6 liters and up suits a living room or a larger open space. A bigger tank also means fewer refills, which for most people is the real reason to step up. The smart models will also stop when the room reaches the humidity you set.

## Keep it clean

A humidifier that sits with standing water is where problems start. The EPA advises emptying the tank, drying it, and refilling with fresh water daily, and cleaning the unit every few days following the maker's instructions. Make it part of the routine, or the humidifier stops being an improvement.

## Worth being direct about

A humidifier changes the moisture in the air. It isn't a treatment for any health condition, whatever a listing title says, so if you're buying one for a medical reason, ask your doctor. Running a humidifier too high can do more harm than dry air, which is why the monitor comes first.
