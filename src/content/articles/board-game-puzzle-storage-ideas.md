Board games and puzzles fail at storage in four distinct ways: the boxes they ship in are flimsy and stack badly, tokens and small pieces end up loose, a puzzle in progress takes over a table indefinitely, and a growing collection eventually outgrows whatever shelf it started on. Each of those is a different problem with a different fix.

## Original boxes are the first thing to replace

Every board game ships in a box sized and shaped a little differently, with corners that crush the first time something heavy gets stacked on top. Moving the actual components — board, cards, pieces — into uniform slim cases fixes the stacking problem immediately and usually saves real shelf space, since a stack of flat boxes is far more efficient than a shelf of mismatched retail boxes standing upright.

## Tokens and small pieces need trays, not sandwich bags

Moving a game out of its box fixes the shelf problem and creates a new one: the punchboard tokens, meeples, and cards that used to sit in the box's cardboard insert now need their own home. Sandwich bags work until one tears. Small stackable trays hold each type of piece separately and can come to the table during play instead of being emptied and refilled every game.

## An unfinished puzzle shouldn't hold a table hostage

The real problem with puzzles isn't storage after they're done — it's the days or weeks a table stays unusable while one is in progress. A roll-up mat solves this directly: roll it up with the puzzle intact, use the table for dinner, unroll it later exactly where you left off. If you'd rather build on a rigid surface, a rotating puzzle board with drawers keeps the puzzle flat and sorts pieces as you go, at the cost of needing a shelf or corner to keep it in.

## A growing collection eventually needs real structure

Slim cases solve the box problem, but a collection that's grown past a dozen or so games needs an actual storage unit, not just a shelf. Pull-out drawers mean the search for one specific game doesn't turn into unstacking half the collection to find it.
