Indoor growing comes in three sizes, and most bad purchases happen when someone buys the wrong one. A countertop garden is a kitchen appliance for herbs and salad greens. A shelf of pots under a grow light is cheaper and grows almost anything small. A grow tent is a real project for vegetables at scale. Decide which of those you actually want first, then buy the system and only the supplies that setup needs.

## Countertop smart gardens: herbs and greens with almost no effort

These combine the light, the reservoir, and the pods in one unit. They're the right choice for someone who wants fresh basil, lettuce, or mint in the kitchen and doesn't want a hobby.

The main fork is soil pods versus water. Click & Grow uses pods with nutrients built into the growing medium, so you add water and nothing else. Hydroponic gardens (LetPot, Ahopegarden, AeroGarden) grow plants with their roots in nutrient water. They grow quickly, but you add nutrients on a schedule and, for the best results, keep an eye on pH.

The other question is control. Click & Grow and Ahopegarden run on preset modes. LetPot and AeroGarden let you set the light schedule from an app.

## A shelf and a grow light: cheaper, and it works with what you already own

If you already have pots and a shelf, a grow light does most of what a countertop garden does for a fraction of the cost, and you're not tied to anyone's pods. Linkable light bars mount under a shelf; a clip-on light covers one or two pots.

A plain grow light needs a timer. Plants grow best on a consistent day length, and nobody remembers to switch a light on and off at the same times every day.

## Grow tents: vegetables at real scale

A tent kit is a contained grow room with its own light, ventilation, and controls. It's the setup for tomatoes, peppers, or a steady harvest of greens through winter. It also takes floor space, uses more power, and needs climate monitoring, so it isn't a first step for someone who has never grown indoors.

## Starting from seed

Seeds sprout faster and more evenly with warmth underneath. A heat mat with a thermostat is the safe choice if it will run while you're not home. A starter tray with a built-in light and humidity dome is the easiest way to raise seedlings before transplanting, or to try indoor growing before buying a full system.

## What hydroponic systems need that soil doesn't

Water-based gardens need two things soil-pod gardens don't. First, nutrients: they come with a starter supply, and a simple one-bottle plant food keeps them going after that. Second, pH: if the water drifts too acidic or too alkaline, plants can't absorb the nutrients even when they're there, and growth stalls. A basic pH kit catches that. A pod kit lets you grow your own seeds instead of buying pre-seeded pods.

## Climate: mainly for tents and seed-starting

In a closed tent or under a humidity dome, temperature and humidity matter. A hygrometer tells you where they are. A small clip fan keeps air moving so moisture doesn't sit on leaves, which helps prevent mold and gives sturdier stems.

## Worth knowing before you buy

- **Pods lock you in.** Countertop gardens are cheap to start and cost more over time if you only use the brand's own pods. Check what refills cost and whether the system takes your own seeds.
- **Hydroponics needs light upkeep.** Topping up water, adding nutrients, and checking pH every week or two is the routine.
- **AeroGarden's history.** The company announced a shutdown for January 2025 and relaunched in spring 2025. The app is still supported, but if long-term brand support matters to you, the app-free options don't depend on it.
