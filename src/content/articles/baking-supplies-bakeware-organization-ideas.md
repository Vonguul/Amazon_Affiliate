A general kitchen-counter organizing fix handles pots, pans, and cutting boards, but it doesn't touch the baking-specific mess: sheet pans stacked loose, a drawer of cookie cutters in every seasonal shape, and measuring cups that never quite nest the way the packaging shows. Baking gear needs its own approach because it's shaped and used differently from everyday cookware.

## Baking pans need to stand, not stack

A stack of flat baking sheets and pans means digging through the whole stack to reach whatever's on the bottom — usually the one pan you actually need. A rack with adjustable dividers holds each pan upright and separate, so any single one slides out without disturbing the rest.

## Cookie cutters are a small-parts problem, not a cookware problem

Dozens of cookie cutters in different seasonal shapes don't fit neatly into any cookware-focused system — they're small, oddly shaped, and easy to lose loose in a drawer. A dedicated compartment box treats them as the small-parts category they actually are, the same logic a junk drawer organizer applies to batteries and twist ties.

## Measuring tools need dividers, not a drawer

Even a "complete" measuring cup and spoon set rarely nests as cleanly in real use as it does in the box — the pieces end up loose and tangled together in a drawer. Dividers keep each size in its own slot, so grabbing the one you need doesn't mean untangling the rest first.

## Bulk flour and sugar need a container, not the bag

A paper bag of flour or sugar that's been opened once never really closes again, and it leaves a trail on the shelf every time it's moved. Airtight canisters sized for a full bag turn the pantry shelf into something you can see into, and mean the scoop goes into the container rather than being pulled out of a torn bag.

## Decorating tips are the smallest part of the problem, and the easiest to lose

If you decorate cakes or cookies, piping tips are tiny enough to disappear into a drawer and delicate enough to bend when they're thrown in loose. A compartment box keeps each tip separate and findable, and it's the same small-parts logic as the cookie cutter box, just at a smaller scale.
