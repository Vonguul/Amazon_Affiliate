A dorm room fails the same organization systems that work everywhere else for one specific reason: it's temporary, shared, and small all at once. Storage has to survive an annual move-out, a hallway walk to a shared bathroom, and a floor plan with almost no spare square footage. The four fixes below are built around that specific set of constraints instead of treating a dorm room like a smaller version of a bedroom.

## Make the walk to a shared bathroom a one-trip job

Nothing in a shared dorm bathroom gets to live there permanently, which means toiletries travel back and forth every single day. A caddy with mesh pockets keeps everything sorted, lets water drain instead of pooling, and turns the whole routine into grabbing one handle instead of gathering loose bottles.

## Pick storage that disappears when it's empty

A dorm room gets packed up completely at least once a year, and rigid storage bins take up exactly as much space empty as they do full — which is a real problem when they have to fit in a car along with everything else. Collapsible crates fold flat for the trip home and pop back into full-size storage the moment they're needed again.

## Give laundry a spot that isn't the floor

A dorm room rarely has room for a permanent hamper, and a pile on the floor is the default when there's no better option. A pop-up hamper folds down to almost nothing when it's not in use and expands in one motion when it is, so it's actually worth keeping instead of skipping.

## Hang things without touching the wall

Most dorm leases don't allow nails or screws, and the security deposit depends on the walls going back the way they came. Adhesive strips and hooks handle coats, mirrors, and lights without a single hole, and pull off clean when it's time to move out.
