Craft supplies fail to stay organized for a specific reason: most of them don't fit neatly into a single bin the way clothes or dishes do. A drawer of screws-sized beads, a shelf of ribbon spools, and a pegboard of scissors all need a different kind of storage, not one bigger box. The six fixes below each target a specific category instead of trying to solve craft storage with a single system.

## Give the whole setup wheels

A dedicated craft table is a luxury most people don't have, which means supplies need to travel to wherever the actual workspace is — the kitchen table, the floor, a shared desk. A rolling cart with drawers moves the whole kit in one trip and rolls back to a corner when the project's done, instead of supplies staying out until someone clears them.

## Small parts need small, labeled slots

A single bin of beads, charms, or findings turns into an unsorted pile within a few projects, and finding the right one means dumping the whole thing out. Dozens of small individual drawers, each holding one type, turn that search into a glance instead of an excavation.

## Ribbon and spools do better upright than loose

A drawer of ribbon spools thrown in loose tangles into itself almost immediately, and the actual colors are hard to see under a pile. Mounted on a rod so each spool spins freely, they stay wound and the whole selection is visible at once.

## Put the tools you use every time on the wall

Scissors, tape, and the small tools used on nearly every project are exactly the things that shouldn't be buried in a drawer — they need to be visible and grabbable. A pegboard uses wall space that's otherwise doing nothing and keeps the most-used tools in constant view instead of lost among less-used supplies.

## Keep paper flat, not curled

Scrapbook paper and cardstock are the supplies that get ruined by storage rather than by use. Stacked loose in a drawer or leaned in a corner, sheets bend, pick up creases, and get bumped at the edges. Flat cases sized for 12x12 sheets keep them level, closed against dust, and easy to carry to a table. They also stack, so paper can be sorted by color, season, or project.

## Give each project its own closed box

Half-finished projects are what make a craft room feel messy, because the pieces sit out until someone finishes. A clear box with a lid and a lift-out tray holds a project's small parts up top and larger materials underneath. It closes up in a few seconds, and the clear sides show which project is inside without opening anything.
