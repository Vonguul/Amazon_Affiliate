Cord clutter isn't actually one problem — it's four or five different ones that look similar because they're all "wires on the floor." A power strip with six things plugged into it, a bundle of parallel cords running to a monitor, and one stray phone charger all need a different fix, and buying one product to handle all of it usually means it does none of them well.

## Start with the power strip, not the cords

Most visible cord chaos traces back to one spot: the power strip everything converges on. Boxing the strip itself — rather than trying to tame every cord individually — solves the worst-looking part of the problem in one step, because the tangle was never really the cords, it was six plugs sitting in the open next to each other.

## Get cords off the floor before bundling them

A cable tray clamped under the desk does something bundling sleeves can't: it gives cords a fixed path to run along, above the floor, instead of hanging down and coiling wherever gravity puts them. This step comes before bundling — sleeves organize cords that already have a route, they don't create one.

## Bundle what runs in parallel, wrap what doesn't

Once cords have a path, group the ones running the same direction — monitor, keyboard, speaker cords headed to the same spot — into a single sleeve. A cord that runs alone, like a phone charger with too much slack, doesn't need a group sleeve; it needs its excess length coiled and held with a reusable tie instead.

## Pin the result down

The last failure point is a cord that was tidied once but slides back down over time because nothing's actually holding it in place. A few adhesive clips at the point where a cord would otherwise sag fixes that permanently, for less than the cost of anything else on this list.
