A spring clean goes faster when you buy for the job in front of you, not the biggest machine on the page. Three machines get confused here. A steam mop is for sealed hard floors, a portable cleaner is for spots, and a full-size carpet cleaner is for whole rooms. Most homes need only one or two of them.

## Sealed hard floors: a steam mop

A steam mop cleans with hot water vapor and a pad, so there's no bucket to carry. The word that matters is "sealed." All three mops here are meant for sealed hard floors, so check your floor maker's care guidance before you start, particularly with hardwood or laminate. If it warns against steam, stick to a regular mop.

Pick by how much mess you get. The Shark S1000 is light and simple. The BISSELL adds steam settings and a flip-down scrubber. The Shark Steam & Scrub does the scrubbing for you, at a higher cost.

## Spots, stairs and furniture: a portable cleaner

If your carpet is mostly fine apart from a few stains, a small spot cleaner is probably all you need. It also reaches what a full-size machine handles badly, such as stairs, chairs and car seats. Because it's quick to bring out, you're more likely to deal with a stain the day it happens.

## Whole rooms: a full-size carpet cleaner

Traffic lanes and dingy rooms need a machine you can push. For apartments and small rooms, a compact model is easier to carry and store. If you have carpet through most of the house, a larger machine with a hose and tools does more per trip. Measure the closet first, because these are the bulkiest things in this guide.

Before you buy, compare the cost of a rental for the one or two times a year you'd use it. Owning makes more sense if you have pets or kids and clean often.

## Carry the small stuff in one trip

A caddy saves more steps than any machine here. Load it once with spray, cloths and gloves, and take it room to room instead of walking back to the closet. ## Worth being direct about

Germ-killing, stain-removal and odor claims on these listings are the makers' own, and they depend on following the directions, so don't treat a steam mop as a medical-grade disinfector. Test any cleaner on a hidden patch of carpet or upholstery first, and follow the fabric's care label. Carpet cleaners also carry a running cost in formula, and the machines put water into the carpet, so leave time for it to dry before anyone walks on it.
