Winter mornings with a car usually go wrong in the same three places: clearing snow off the car takes too long, the windshield is iced over, and the driveway between you and the road needs digging out. Each has a simple fix, and together they fit in a trunk and a garage corner.

## Clear the car, don't just brush it

A wide foam head on a telescoping pole moves snow off a roof in a few pushes, and reaching the middle of the roof matters: snow left up there slides onto the windshield the first time you brake. Keep one brush in the car and a second by the door if you have two cars.

## Or skip scraping completely

A windshield cover is the cheapest time saver in this guide. It goes on the night before, and in the morning the frost and snow come off with it. It also stops the wipers freezing to the glass.

## Move snow without hurting yourself

Shoveling is genuinely strenuous work. A snow pusher moves light and medium snow without lifting at all. For heavy, wet snow, a shovel with a second handle lets you lift closer to the load with your legs. Take breaks, and if you have a heart condition, ask your doctor about shoveling before the first storm.

## Melt ice without damaging the concrete

Rock salt works, but it can pit concrete, especially newer concrete, and it irritates dogs' paws. Blends sold as safer for concrete, and salt-free melts for pet homes, are the gentler options. Whatever you use, follow the amount on the bag.

## Where it all lives

Brush and windshield cover in the trunk, blankets in the glovebox, shovels and ice melt in a garage or entry corner where you can grab them before you need to step outside. For the rest of the trunk, see [car trunk organization](/articles/car-trunk-organization-ideas/), and for wet boots and gloves by the door, see [winter gear storage](/articles/winter-gear-storage-ideas/).

## Worth being direct about

"No-scratch", "safer for concrete" and "pet safe" are the makers' descriptions, not independent test results. Grit trapped in any brush can scratch paint, so knock the brush clean before you use it. And no gadget replaces checking the forecast: put the windshield cover on and the ice melt by the door before a storm, not during it.
