Most "privacy gadget" advice online skips the part where it explains what the product actually stops. Every item below is matched to a specific, documented risk — card skimming, a camera left active without you knowing, signal tracking, phishing, and a planted tracker — rather than a vague sense of being watched.

## Skimming a contactless card is a real attack, not a myth

Researchers have demonstrated reading a contactless card's data from several feet away using cheap, easily available equipment. An RFID-blocking wallet doesn't require doing anything differently — it just physically shields the cards it's already holding, the same way it would if the wallet were simply metal-lined instead of leather.

## Cover the lens instead of trusting the light

A webcam's "recording" indicator light is controlled by software, and software that's been compromised can control it too — meaning the light isn't a reliable guarantee the camera is actually off. A physical slide cover removes the ambiguity: closed means blocked, full stop, regardless of what any app claims.

## Airplane mode is a setting, not a hardware guarantee

Airplane mode is a toggle the phone's own software manages, which means malware with enough access can silently override it. A Faraday bag doesn't ask the phone to cooperate — it physically blocks every radio signal in or out, which is the only way to be certain a phone is actually unreachable, not just told to say it is.

## A password can be phished. A hardware key can't be typed into a fake page

Phishing works by tricking someone into typing a password or a one-time code into a page that looks real. A hardware security key breaks that trick at the root: the cryptographic proof of identity happens on the physical device and is tied to the real site's actual address, so even a perfect fake login page has nothing to steal.

## Do a fast check before assuming a rental is private

Hidden cameras and trackers in short-term rentals and rental cars are a documented, recurring problem, and most people have no way to check beyond a visual scan of obvious hiding spots. A handheld RF/Bluetooth detector finds the active signal a hidden device gives off, catching what a visual check alone would miss.
