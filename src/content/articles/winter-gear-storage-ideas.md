Winter adds a second wardrobe to the front door. Every person in the house now has a hat, gloves, a scarf, and wet boots, all coming off in the same few square feet, several times a day. The mess isn't because anyone is untidy. There's simply nowhere for these things to go.

Three questions sort it out: where does each small thing live, how does wet gear get dry, and where do the spares go?

## Give every person a pocket

Hats and gloves get lost because they're small and go into a shared pile. A pocket organizer on the back of the closet or entry door fixes that: one row per person, so a missing glove has exactly one place it can be. Mesh pockets let you see inside and let damp things air.

Hats that are worn daily can go on hooks instead, where they're faster to grab.

## Dry the wet gear, don't just park it

A boot tray protects the floor, but boots sitting on a tray stay damp inside, and damp boots are cold and start to smell. A forced-air dryer solves the actual problem by drying them from the inside overnight. It also handles gloves, which is the item kids most often put on wet.

The tray still matters for the melt and the salt. For a household, size it for everyone's boots at once, or the overflow ends up on the floor anyway.

## Scarves need to hang

Folded scarves take a drawer and still wrinkle. A multi-bar hanger puts all of them on one bit of closet rod where you can see each one.

## Keep spares out of the daily spot

The entry only works if it holds what's used this week. Spare gloves, heavy scarves, and ski gear go in stackable bins on a closet shelf. Bins with a window save you from opening every one to find the snow mittens.

## At the end of the season

Wash and fully dry everything before storing it. Boots and gloves put away damp are the usual cause of a musty closet in autumn.
