Small bathrooms lose usable storage to three spots almost every time: the cabinet under the sink (wasted to plumbing and clutter), the wall above the toilet (usually just... blank wall), and the shower itself (bottles balanced on the tub edge). None of these need a renovation to fix — they need the right small, install-in-minutes product.

## Under the sink is almost always wasted space

The cabinet under a bathroom sink looks small, but most of what's "lost" is depth — items get pushed to the back and forgotten because nothing's visible past the first row. Pull-out organizer drawers fix this by making the entire depth of the cabinet accessible without kneeling down and digging.

## The wall above the toilet is free real estate

It's one of the most overlooked spots in a small bathroom, mostly because people assume adding storage there means mounting shelves into tile. A freestanding over-the-toilet unit straddles the toilet itself and needs no wall mounting at all — just make sure whatever you pick has anti-tip bracing, since these units are top-heavy by design.

## The shower doesn't need built-in shelving

Bottles balanced on a tub ledge or hung from a flimsy wire caddy over the shower head are both common failure points. An adhesive-mounted corner caddy holds real weight without drilling into tile, and it comes off cleanly if you're renting — worth checking the weight rating before loading it up with full-size bottles.

## Fix the biggest failure point first

If you can only fix one thing, fix the under-sink cabinet first — it's usually the largest single chunk of wasted storage in a small bathroom, and the one people avoid dealing with because it means getting on the floor to sort through what's back there.
