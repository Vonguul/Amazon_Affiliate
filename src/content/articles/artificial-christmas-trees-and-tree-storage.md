A good artificial tree is a ten-year purchase, so the two decisions that matter are made before you buy: whether you want it pre-lit, and where it will live for the other eleven months.

## Measure first: height and width

Take your ceiling height and subtract about a foot for the stand and the topper. That's your tallest tree. An 8-foot ceiling suits a 7.5-ft tree with a slim topper, or a 7-ft tree with a star.

Then check the width. A full 7.5-ft tree is often 4 to 5 feet across at the base, which is more floor than most people expect. If the spot is a corner or a narrow gap beside a sofa, a pencil tree gives you the height without the footprint.

## Pre-lit or unlit

Pre-lit trees save the most annoying hour of the season: no stringing lights around every branch. The trade-off is that the lights are built in. When a section goes dark years from now, it's harder to fix than swapping a strand. Trees wired so the rest stay lit when one bulb fails, like the Puleo trees here, make that less likely to ruin a season.

Unlit trees cost less for the same quality of branches and let you choose your own lights, but you string them every year. If you already own good lights, unlit is the better value.

## Fluffing is the real setup time

Every artificial tree ships compressed. Shaping each branch, bottom to top, is what makes a tree look full, and it takes longer than assembly. Gloves help: the needles are stiff. Hinged branches, which the Balsam Hill, Puleo and Best Choice trees have, drop into place on their own and cut the time down.

## Storage decides whether it lasts

Trees get crushed and bent in storage, not in use. The cardboard box a tree arrives in rarely survives a second year.

- **Store it standing:** an upright bag slips over the assembled tree, so next year you skip assembly and most of the fluffing. It needs a closet or garage corner tall enough to hold it.
- **Store it in pieces:** a wheeled duffel holds the sections flat and rolls to the garage or under the stairs.
