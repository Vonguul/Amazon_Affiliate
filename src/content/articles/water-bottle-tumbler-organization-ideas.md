The cabinet that won't close is almost never actually short on space — it's short on structure. A dozen water bottles and tumblers stacked loose take up far more room than the same dozen standing upright in individual slots, and the lids and straws that come off them scatter to wherever there's room, usually a totally different drawer.

## Bottles need slots, not a shelf

A bottle set on a bare shelf has nothing to lean against, so it falls the moment a neighboring bottle is pulled out — which is exactly the domino effect that turns a cabinet into chaos every time someone grabs a water bottle. Individual slots solve this directly: each bottle stays upright on its own, independent of what happens next to it.

## Lids and straws need to travel with their bottle

A lid or straw removed for washing rarely makes it back to the exact bottle it came from — it ends up in a drawer with unrelated kitchen tools instead. An organizer with a dedicated accessory compartment keeps that pairing intact, so the parts for one bottle don't slowly disperse across the kitchen.

## Straws specifically need their own dense storage

A household with several people each using their own tumbler accumulates straws faster than any general accessory bin accounts for. A dedicated door-mounted holder uses space that's otherwise doing nothing — the inside of a cabinet door — and keeps straws upright and easy to grab one at a time.

## Lids sort better standing up than stacked flat

A stack of loose lids in a drawer means checking each one by hand to find the size that fits today's tumbler. A slotted lid organizer keeps them upright and separated, so the right one is visible at a glance instead of buried in a pile.
